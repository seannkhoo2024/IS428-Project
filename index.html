Why this matters

Choosing a degree is one of the biggest financial decisions a family makes, and the data to compare options is scattered.

  • No tool compares like with like

    The Graduate Employment Survey is published as long yearly tables. There is no easy way to put similar courses at different universities side by side.

  • University averages hide programmes

    A university's median depends on its mix of programmes. The same discipline can pay very differently from one campus to the next.

  • One year is not a trend

    Outcomes move with each cohort and with inflation. A strong year can look like a lasting advantage when it isn't.

We go further than existing GES tools: thirteen cohorts (2013–2025) in real 2024 dollars, the P25–P75 range and not only the median, reliability flags on every number, and tuition fees for five student groups.

Chapter 1 · The puzzle

Graduates earn more, but fewer land permanent jobs

Real median starting pay rose from S$3,577 to S$4,430 between 2013 and 2025. Yet the share in full-time permanent jobs fell to 73.4% in 2025, the lowest since the COVID year.

What this shows
Two tiles give the 2025 median pay and full-time permanent rate. The line traces real median starting pay from 2013 to 2025. The stacked area splits employment into full-time permanent and other jobs, with the overall employment rate as a line.
Key insight
Pay rose 23.9% in real terms, with a dip in 2021 (S$4,034). The permanent rate bottomed at 69.0% in 2020, recovered to 88.6% in 2022, then fell to 73.4%. Overall employment was 94.7% in 2020 but only 83.3% in 2025: in 2020 most graduates found some job, in 2025 fewer found any job within six months.
Why it matters
The "average graduate" story hides a job-security squeeze, and it is broad-based: every university's median permanent rate fell from 2022 to 2025 (NUS 91.4% to 79.1%, NTU 87.1% to 71.4%, SUSS 83.3% to 69.7%). The rest of the site looks at who is affected.

How to use: hover over any year for the exact figures. This page has no filters.

Chapter 2 · What you study

Field sets the floor, but not the ceiling

Information & Digital Technologies has topped the field ranking every year, while Health Sciences slid from 2nd to 8th. But inside the top fields, programmes differ by more than S$2,000 a month (IDT: S$4,464–6,842; Business: S$3,446–5,907).

What this shows
The bump chart ranks fields by median starting pay each year (fields with three or more programmes that year). The strip plot places every 2025 programme in its field, coloured by university, with a dashed line at the field median.
Key insight
Information & Digital Technologies ranks 1st in every year from 2013 to 2025 (2025 field median S$4,996), and Engineering is 2nd in 11 of 13 years. Health Sciences fell from 2nd (2014–2016) to 8th in 2025. Arts, Design & Media stays near the bottom (S$3,741).
Why it matters
The spread inside a field is wider than the gap between most field medians (S$3,741–4,996). Choosing a field is necessary but not sufficient; the specific programme matters, as Altonji, Blom & Meghir (2012) found for college majors.

How to use: click a field in the ranking to highlight its programmes in the strip plot.

Chapter 3 · Where you study

The same degree can pay very differently

In 2025 Computer Science graduates' median pay ranged from S$4,489 to S$6,301 depending on the programme and university, a 40% gap.

What this shows
Median pay by field and university; a dot-and-range for each programme in the chosen field and year (P25, median, P75) with its full-time permanent rate; and diverging bars for each programme's median minus the field median, blue above and orange below.
Key insight
Computer Science runs from S$4,489 (SIT, CS in Interactive Media & Game Development) to S$6,301 (NUS), a 40% gap. Business spans S$4,430–5,907 (33%) and Computer Engineering S$4,464–5,513 (24%). The top 2025 programme is NTU's Business & Computing double degree at S$6,842.
Why it matters
University medians (SUTD S$4,868, NUS S$4,568, SMU S$4,480, NTU S$4,430, SIT S$4,184, SUSS S$3,938) hide programme mix: SIT's game-design CS pays like an arts degree, while its simulation CS (S$4,922) matches SUTD. Compare within a field or discipline.

How to use: pick a Field and Year, or click a field in the first chart to switch to it.

Chapter 4 · Trade-offs

High pay, job security and predictability rarely come together

Pay and permanent-employment rates are only weakly linked, and the best-paid programmes also have the widest spread of outcomes.

What this shows
A quadrant chart of full-time permanent rate against median pay (bubble size is graduates; reference lines at the year's medians), and a scatter of pay spread (IQR ÷ median) against median pay.
Key insight
The 118 programmes in 2025 split almost evenly: 32 high pay and high employment, 30 high pay with lower employment, 27 lower pay with high employment, 29 lower on both. The correlation between pay and permanent rate is only r = 0.29.
Why it matters
Better-paid programmes tend to have a wider relative spread (r = 0.48); NUS Business Administration's IQR is 83% of its median, so a high median is no guarantee. The lowest permanent rates: NTU Art, Design & Media 34.8%, NUS Landscape Architecture 40.0%.

How to use: choose a Year, then click a programme to highlight it in both charts.

Chapter 5 · What it costs

Who you are changes the payback more than what you study

A citizen needs about 7 months of median starting pay to cover tuition; a non-subsidised student needs about 33.

What this shows
Median total tuition by student group, payback months for each programme in the chosen group and field, a fee ladder comparing the five groups' fees per programme, and a value map of pay against payback.
Key insight
Median total tuition runs from S$32,129 for citizens to S$150,885 for non-subsidised students. Median payback is 7.4 months (citizen), 10.7 (PR), 16.4 (ASEAN), 19.0 (other international) and 32.5 (non-subsidised). For citizens the field gap is far smaller: 6.2 months (IDT) to 9.0 (Arts, Design & Media), excluding Dentistry at 28.3.
Why it matters
Same pay, different payback: SMU Computer Science and NUS Information Systems both pay S$5,907, but citizen payback is 7.6 vs 5.4 months. For international and non-subsidised students, the cost side can outweigh pay differences between programmes.

How to use: choose your Student group and a Field.

Payback months = total tuition ÷ median gross monthly starting pay, both in real 2024 S$. It uses 2025 salaries with AY2026/27 fees, and ignores taxes, CPF, living costs, financial aid, loans and pay growth.

Chapter 6 · Does it last?

Gaps widen, leaders persist, and supply is not destiny

The gap between programmes has nearly tripled since 2013, a handful of programmes beat their field every single year, and growing intakes have not pushed pay down.

What this shows
A box plot of programme medians for each year; a slope chart of real pay between two chosen cohorts for the selected field; the share of years each programme beat its field median; and change in graduates against change in real pay.
Key insight
The interquartile range of programme medians grew from S$233 (2013) to S$664 (2025), peaking at S$929 in 2022. From 2015 to 2025 the median programme's real pay rose 14.6% and only 6% of programmes fell. NUS Computer Science, NUS Business Administration (Hons) and NTU Accountancy & Business beat their field in all 13 years.
Why it matters
Across 53 programmes, change in graduates and change in real pay are essentially unrelated (r = −0.11): median graduates rose 6% and median pay 15%. Growing a programme has not visibly pushed its starting pay down. (Single-programme fields such as Dentistry always equal their own median, so "beats field" does not apply there.)

How to use: choose a Field and the start and end cohorts; click a dot in the supply chart to switch field.

Chapter 7 · Your programme

Your programme, in one screen

Pick a programme, your student group and a year. Click any peer to compare.

How to read the profile chart

The parallel-coordinates chart gives each programme one line across five axes: pay, permanent job, pay certainty, pay growth and affordability. Your programme is the dark line; every other programme is a light line behind it.

Each axis runs from 0% (the lowest programme that year) to 100% (the highest). Pay certainty and affordability are flipped so that up is better on every axis: a narrow pay range and a short payback both sit near the top.

Example: NUS Computer Science for a citizen in 2025 shows S$6,301 median pay, 84.1% in full-time permanent jobs, +57.6% real pay since its first survey and a 5.1-month payback. Clicking SMU Computer Science switches the card to S$5,907 and 83.3%.

How to use: choose a Programme, Student group and Year. Click any peer in the head-to-head or trend chart to switch the whole card to that programme. The reliability tile warns you when a number rests on a small sample, and "Not linked" means MOM graduate counts are not available (most NUS and SUSS programmes).

Key takeaways

Six things we'd want a student and their parents to know, in the order the story tells them.

  1. Real starting pay is up about a quarter since 2013, but full-time permanent employment is at its lowest since 2020 (73.4%).

  2. Field matters (IDT leads every year; Health Sciences slid from 2nd to 8th), but the spread within a field is larger than the gaps between most fields.

  3. The same discipline can differ by 25–40% across universities (Computer Science: 40%).

  4. Pay and job security are only weakly related (r = 0.29), and higher pay comes with a wider spread.

  5. Fees by student group change payback roughly fourfold (7.4 to 32.5 months), more than field choice does for citizens.

  6. Gaps between programmes have widened, a small set of programmes beat their field every year, and graduate supply has not visibly depressed pay.

Recommendations

Prospective students and parents

  • Compare within a discipline, not by university average.
  • Check full-time permanent rates and the P25–P75 range, not only the median.
  • Use payback for your own student group.
  • Treat programmes flagged small-sample or not reported with caution.

Universities and MOE

  • Publish GES with response counts for every row, as Table T15 began doing in 2025.
  • Keep a consistent programme identifier across years.
  • Publish P10 and P90 if possible.
  • Report fees alongside outcomes.

Future versions of this tool

  • Add polytechnic GES for diploma-versus-degree context.
  • Add longer-run outcomes when they become available.
  • Offer national wage benchmarks as optional reference lines.

Data sources
  • Graduate Employment Survey 2013–2024 for NTU, NUS, SIT, SMU, SUSS and SUTD (MOE via data.gov.sg): 1,550 rows of employment rates and salary percentiles per degree.
  • GES 2025, Table T15 (MOM): 148 degree rows from six university sheets, adding response rate and "secured employment".
  • Graduates from Institutions of Higher Learning by Institution and Course, 2015–2025 (MOM): graduate counts per course.
  • Consumer Price Index, 2024 = 100, monthly (SingStat): for deflating salaries and fees.
  • AY2026/27 tuition fee schedules from each university's fee pages: 140 of 150 current programmes mapped.
  • Labour Force Survey income tables (MOM/SingStat): prepared as national wage benchmarks, used as context only.

The cleaned main table has 1,615 rows (programme × year), 2013–2025, covering 183 programmes, 96 disciplines and 14 fields.

Cleaning and the course crosswalk

A Python pipeline rebuilds every table from the seven source files in one command, with logs and QA files as an audit trail. It repairs garbled school names, separates footnote symbols from degree titles and turns them into flags (small sample, not reported, follow-up survey, merged programme), and converts 928 "na" cells to blanks.

A crosswalk maps 458 raw degree titles to 183 canonical programmes, 96 disciplines and 14 fields (the MOE GES 2025 course clusters), with one explicit rule per title. Pure renamings are merged into one series. Anything that ran in the same year as its look-alike stays separate, for example NUS Honours and non-Honours, or SIT Chemical Engineering with Newcastle and with TU Munich.

Checks: exactly one row per programme per year, one field and discipline per programme, and all 1,480 cells of the 2025 rows verified against Table T15 with no mismatches.

Real dollars: how we adjust for inflation

Every salary is converted to 2024 prices: real = nominal × CPI(2024 average) ÷ CPI(survey month), using CPI All Items for the month each university ran its survey (November for NUS, NTU, SMU and SUSS; March of the following year for SIT; February of the following year for SUTD until it moved to November in 2024; November 2025 for all six in 2025).

Using the survey month rather than the annual average changes values by a median of 0.39% and up to 4.29%. Tuition is deflated with August 2026 CPI, the start of AY2026/27.

Assumptions
  1. Employment prospects are GES outcomes about six months after graduation. The main measures are the full-time permanent employment rate and gross monthly salary.
  2. All money is in real 2024 dollars.
  3. Outcomes are distributions: each programme-year is shown with its median, P25 and P75.
  4. Comparisons across universities are made within a field or discipline.
  5. Small samples and follow-up surveys are handled separately. Rows with fewer than 30 respondents are left out of headline views. Law, Medicine, Pharmacy, Architecture and BMS & Chinese Medicine are surveyed later and excluded from fresh-graduate charts.
  6. National occupational wages are a field-level reference only and are never used for ranking.
  7. Student groups follow the universities' fee categories: Singapore Citizen, Permanent Resident, International (ASEAN), International (Other) and Non-subsidised (no Tuition Grant).
Payback definition

Payback months = total tuition (real) ÷ median gross monthly starting pay (real). It is a simple affordability ratio: how many months of a median starting salary equal the whole tuition bill.

Payback uses 2025 salaries only, because the fees are AY2026/27 fees. It ignores taxes, CPF, living costs, bursaries and other aid, loans and salary growth. Fees are each university's annual fee × programme length (SUSS: programme total; SIT: fee per credit × total credits).

Limitations
  • GES measures outcomes about six months after graduation: labour-market entry, not careers. Response rates are below 100% and non-respondents are not profiled.
  • Distributions are coarse: GES publishes only P25, median and P75.
  • Comparisons are descriptive, not causal. Programmes differ in who they admit (Altonji, Blom & Meghir, 2012).
  • MOM counts are graduates, not respondents; 2025 respondent counts are estimates; NUS and SUSS counts are mostly unlinkable.
  • Payback ignores taxes, CPF, living costs, financial aid, loans and salary growth, and applies AY2026/27 fees to 2025 salaries.
  • A few crosswalk judgement calls (all documented) can change individual series.
  • Wage benchmarks are context only. Polytechnic GES is not included yet.
